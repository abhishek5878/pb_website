<p class="thesis">People who can do a hundred times the work are offered two doors: start a company, or take a job. Many of them need a third.</p>

That was the thesis in February 2026. Talent misallocation is the biggest capital inefficiency there is. When someone who can do 100x work sits in a 1x role, the loss isn't additive. It's exponential.

## What I believed

**The binary is the waste.** Build a company, or get a job. Those two doors fit most people and fail the rare ones.

**Work done in public is truer than a resume.** Code, writing, arguments on forums, preprints: what someone actually did, not what they say they did.

**A person may not know their own worth.** So Sentinel had two faces. One for investors, to find the outliers months before they hit the open market. One for the person themselves: paste a link to your work, and see the gap between where you are and where you'd fit.

**High-agency human talent is the only remaining alpha.** And the spec's bar for the product: the first thing that makes people gasp when they use it.

## What it taught me

- **It narrowed as I built.** Four paths for a person became three, with founders at the centre.
- **It never met a user.** Every profile in it was invented, and it was never deployed.

I built it over about three weeks, from the memo on 25 February to 13 March 2026. The next steps were a real database and a deploy. Neither happened.
