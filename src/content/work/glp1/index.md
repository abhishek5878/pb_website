<p class="thesis">When a drug gets cheap, the hard part moves to the care around it.</p>

Care for GLP-1 patients in India after the patent expires. Once anyone can afford the drug, the question stops being who can get it and becomes who stays on it, safely.

## What I believed

**Care, not commerce.** A chronic-care layer around the patient. Not a pharmacy, and not a doctor marketplace.

**Staying on the drug is the real problem.** The work is the months after the first prescription: the dose steps, the side effects, the check-ins.

**Indian bodies and Indian plates are different.** Different cut-offs, rice and roti at their own times of day, and the family at the table. The family at the table matters as much as the prescription.

**Safety comes before code.** I set three rules before writing any product: no dosing protocol without a named clinician behind it, made-up patients only for the first version, and anonymisation reviewed before any dashboard.

## What it taught me

- **The plan named its own weak point.** If the family and the diet don't change how many people stay on the drug, the whole thing stops making sense.
- **There was no clinician partner yet.** And the first rule said nothing starts without one.
- **Nothing was ever built for patients.** I opened the project on 28 April 2026, the day after the research, and it never got past the plan.
