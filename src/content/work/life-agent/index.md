<p class="thesis">Ramu Kaka for every family.</p>

Ramu Kaka is the one every film family has: he has been around forever, he knows everyone, and things get done because he's there. Most families don't have one.

The doctor said something; nobody followed up. The bank sent a notice; nobody understood it. The school had a deadline; nobody caught it. This is not a knowledge problem. Indians are not uninformed. It is an implementation gap: the chasm between receiving information and completing the action it demands.

Close the gap between information and implementation. For every family. One by one.

## What I believed

**It joins the group the family already has.** No new app, no new behaviour. It is the +1 in the family WhatsApp group.

**Trust is sequenced, and silence comes first.** Weeks of only watching. Then only reminders about time. Then information, never recommendations. Only then, doing things. The agent cannot say the wrong thing if it doesn't speak.

**Who pays decides whom it serves.** An agent that earns from subscriptions is a fiduciary.

**Win Mom first.** The logistics veto is the real veto.

**Limits are rules, not a model's judgement.** Quiet hours and daily caps on interruptions are fixed, not learned.

## What it taught me

- **I designed the family before meeting one.** My first household model hard-coded one shape of family, and I archived it the next day. The shape has to come from real families, not from my idea of them.
- **A clever model can argue its way around a rule.** "Rules below, intelligence above" had to go: a model can relabel reality until the guardrail no longer applies.
- **The thesis could not be sharpened further without contact with reality.** The roadmap said so itself: the most expensive thing we can do is keep writing.

The next step was a four-week pilot with a person playing the agent by hand. It never started.
