<p class="thesis">How are you, really?</p>

That is the question on the bench, in Iti's words. Above it, smaller, in mine: *Come sit with me.*

Signal Spark is a gym for the mind that never shows you improving. You come when you need something: some words, some frame of mind, or when you can't get out of a frame of mind. You take one thing, and you leave. A single piece can be the whole visit. The program is a repertoire, not a calendar.

I'm building it with Iti Bhargava, who runs [Signal Studio](https://signalstudio.in). Most of the words in it, the two of us write by hand.

## What we believe

**You can keep a sense of self with evidence you chose.** On a bad night, the self stops being a credible witness on its own behalf. So Signal Spark keeps a private box of what reminds you of your worth: a photo, a song, a friend's message, a letter sealed for a hard day. In Iti's words, every bubble you see is a piece of evidence you chose to keep.

**Naming a feeling is the first help, not a form.** The way in is the bench and that one question. You can write, or show where you feel it, and then we don't say anything. We let you speak for a while.

**No machine in the other seat.** I have many friends who ask me for advice about their own mental health. I sense something between their lines. I don't want our voice or our agent to be the one answering that, but it should help them find their way there. Nothing you type is read, scored or reacted to, and nothing tells you what you really feel. I'm not trying to replace therapists. I really am not.

**Wanted back, never pulled.** No streaks, no notifications, no score, no feed. Even our own analytics is held to a rule: nothing it proposes may aim at more use. Iti put it best: you should *want* to return, not be *pulled* back.

**Say only what is earned.** The crisis lines are on every screen. Iti's own introduction, in her recorded voice, tells you that a month later the effects in the research were small, so the gym is a practice you come back to, not a one-time fix.

**Help before membership.** The first minutes should give you something before they ask you for anything, and the voice at the door should be a human one. The introduction is already Iti's own recorded voice.

## What it is, for the person on the bench

A bench where you say how you are, and it lets go of it by default. A next step that fits the words you chose: an exercise, or one of your own reminders. A box that hands you one thing when browsing is too much. A gym of exercises under ten minutes, each with an ordinary example, a private try, and one line to carry out with you. A "right now" page with one reminder, one person to message, and the helplines. And a way out on every screen: *That's enough for now.*

## What it taught us

- **Our founding bet came back mixed, and against us for the worst night.** Keeping reminders of your worth can help, but positive recall can backfire exactly when things are at their hardest.
- **In the whole evidence base, the large and lasting effects come with a human, even a thin one.** That is why Iti's voice is at the door, and why a bench with trained listeners is where this goes later.
- **The words have to sound like two tired humans.** Early on, Iti said they felt AI-generated. We rewrote them ourselves, round after round. My first try at the gym's opening question had three sentences with the word "shitty" in them; the same day it was four shorter ones: *It's me. It's the situation. It's coming out on others. I'm keeping it in.*
- **The first five minutes asked too much and explained too little.** That came from the first people Iti shared it with, and it is why the next release puts help before any card.
- **Nothing proves it works yet, and nothing can yet.** We say so.

## Where it is going

It's live, free, for anyone 18 and over in India, and Iti shares it person to person. Next: help before signing up, a preview before you commit, and a consented study Iti sets up, so one day we can say what it did. Later, a bench where a trained volunteer can sit with you. A community only once this spark has lit something.

Every word is still a draft until it has been through clinical review. It is not therapy, and it is not for a crisis.
