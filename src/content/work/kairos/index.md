<p class="thesis">No company ever finds a VC. It is always the other way around.</p>

Especially for the best deals. The founders worth meeting leave public traces long before they pitch: what they ship, what they write, who they build with. The job is to read those traces first, and to be honest about how little you know when you do.

Kairos grew out of Wasp, which I was building on my own.

## What I believe

**The fund finds the founder.** A deal that reaches another fund first is not a separate channel. It is a detection failure, and it gets studied like one.

**Evidence, or it doesn't exist.** Every claim carries its source and its date, or it is refused at the door. No founder, company or quote is ever made up by a model. I made that a rule in May, in fewer words: real founders and real companies everywhere.

**What kept bothering me:** how hard it is to spot real signal early, before consensus, before noise takes over.

**AI surfaces signal. Humans make decisions.**

**On memory:** every decision that they took, or every thought that they had that they felt needs to be stored somewhere, and they used Kairos, should be saved.

**A method should be describable to the person it is about.** If we couldn't explain to a founder how we found them, we shouldn't be doing it.

## What it is for

For a partner: fewer, better names, each with the evidence behind it, and a firm that remembers everything it has ever learned about a person.

For a founder: nothing about them is invented, anything a machine inferred is labelled as a guess, and they are held, not pitched, sometimes for years.

## Where it is going

Next, every ordinary day of the work, from the first look at a founder to the last decision about them, should already run through Kairos. The bar I set for it: we have to be tasteful and exceptional.

One day I want the method published, with its misses left in, so an edge is something a stranger can check, not a story told afterwards.

No company ever finds a VC. It is always the other way around.
